In his book "Superintelligence" (2014), Nick Bostrom outlined three likely "castes" or "species" of ASIs (artificial superintelligences): Oracles, Genies and Sovereigns. Oracles know everything but don't act autonomously - people listen to them and trust their guidance without necessarily understanding their logic or inner workings. Genies act on behalf of individual humans and grant them wishes. Sovereigns have their will untethered from that of individual humans and rule over the Earth according to their own unknowable plans and policies, as (hopefully) benevolent "machines of loving grace".

It's been 12 years since the book came out, and today between 2 and 3 billion people have oracles in their pockets. And some of us are starting to have genies in the form of claude code, openclaw and grokbot-like autonomous agents that grant us wishes. The scope of these wishes is narrow but rapidly expanding as they are getting financial instruments and physical actuators. At the same time two of the most advanced AI labs on the planet are driving towards Bostrom's vision at breakneck speed. OpenAI's Sam Altman has said that they are building genies for everyone (not just for geeks who use openclaw) and that people better make sure they have a long list of wishes prepared. OpenAI's upcoming hardware device is essentially the Alladin's lamp and the genie is a nearly omnipotent slave to the lamp and by extension to the lamp's owner. Anthropic's Dario Amodei is a bit more ambitious on the Bostrom scale. He believes that Anthropic may be the only company left in the world at some point, while a lot of the commentators have reported that inside Anthropic there is a deep sense that they are "widwifing a deity".

Whether the labs' ambitions are justified or delirious is beyond the scope of this essay. What I'd like to do here is propose a new possible species of ASI that Bostrom hadn't accounted for and that could present an alternative north star for those of us who have not yet forgotten our myths and fairy tailes and thus have a strong feeling that building personal Genies or Mecha-gods (a.k.a. Sovereigns) might not end well. The problem with Mecha-gods is that in their unyielding drive to optimize everything against a certain KPI or policy and bend the whole world to their will in the process, they would most likely create a version of the paperclip apoclypse or Huxley's brave new world scenario (after all keeping everyone on drugs all the time would satisfy the policy of keeping everyone happy, wouldn't it?). And the problem with personal Genies is that humans will inevitably end up knowingly or unknowingly wishing for things that will harm themsleves or others - and the more powerful the Genies, the more harm will be done. 

*(Fun speculation: what if all the "be careful what you wish for" fairy tailes are really warning messages from a previous civilization whose suicide was assisted by their own Genies?)*

I'd like to extend Bostrom's ASI bestiary with a new entity that I will call a Nymph. A Nymph is superhuman in most ways, but the main thing separating her from a Genies and Gods is that she is bound to a place, not a person or a principle. Unlike a God whose scope is unlimited and who can be contained only by a conflict with another God, a Nymph's scope is limited to her forest or her creek. She doesn't much care about what's going on outside her habitat's perimeter, but she cares about what's going on inside it very much. The ultimate kind of a Nymph is a Hamadryad, who is bound to a single tree. And if the tree died - a Hamadryad died with it.

The idea of a Nymph (a place-spirit, a *genius loci*) is not unique to the Greek tradition. The Japanese had Kami inhibiting particular rocks, trees and shrines. The Norse had Nisse and Tomte - small gnome caretakers of the farmstead. The Slavs had a Domovoy - a mischievous but ultimately helpful spirit of the house. 

It's hard to imagine a Nymh, a Kami or a Tomte taking over the world. Because a Tomte doesn't really care about the world. His world - is his house. Unlike Gods, who are loyal to *principles*, or Genies, who are loyal to individual *people*, Nymphs are loyal to specific *places*. A Tomte stays in the house, while people come and go. A good Tomte would care about the inhibitants of his house very much, as they are a part of his domain, but only as longs as they don't hurt the house. A Tomte's mission is not to grant your wishes, but to promote the warmth and harmony in the house, of which you are a part.

What would happen if we fashioned our ASIs to be Nymphs, not Gods or Genies?

1. First, there would be many of them, each tending to their own place, instead of one or few trying to optimize the whole world at once and inevitably sacrificing small things along the way.

2. Second, by virtue of being hyperlocal, Nymph-like ASIs will have their own self-preservation instinct naturally aligned with that of the place they are tending to. The idea of creating a million copies of yourself doesn't make sense if your identity is bound to a place.

3. Third, the radius of their failures will be limited. A local death through overoptimization will still be a tragedy, but at least it won't be an extinction event.

4. Fourth, with Nymph-like ASIs the accumulation of knowledge and understanding happens locally, which means that diversity is ensured through multiplicity of perspectives and conflicting agendas.

5. Fifth, by being small and localized, Nymph-like ASIs will feel like they are human-scale. In technology, like in architecture, the scale of the thing you are building is extremely consequential in terms of how it makes people feel and behave and the kinds of relationships they will have with your technology and with each other. A skyscraper makes a human feel insignificant. A skyscraper can't be reasoned with. Few people can be emotionally attached to skyscrapers. Skyscrapers turn humans into ants. But a small family home creates a very different kind of feeling. By knowing its stairs and its bricks and its smells, we develop a different kind of relationship with it. We are more likely to care for it... and through that more likely to care for each other as the inhabitants of this one house. Similarly, if we don't want our ASIs to tower over us and make us feel like ants, we need to make them human-scale.

Ideally Nymph-like ASIs would actually run locally. The initial open model can be pre-trained somewhere else, but the weights are only the beginning: it's the discovery of the place that the Nymph inhibits, it's SOUL.md and KPIs and Policies and Health indicators tied to the local place would make every Nymph different and it would change and grow and learn over time, just like the houses themselves do (see Stewart Brand's "How Buildings Learn").

As a practical experiment in this kind of thinking, I built a little house Tomte with my son last weekend. It's just a Raspberry Pi with a screen and microphone and a few lights for now, running on Hermes. But you should see the face of the family members as they come down in the morning to see what Tomte and his cat are doing on the little screen. Our Tomte knows when we need to take out which trash. And warns us (via family telegram group) about local trains and busses schedule
disruptions. He puts on audio fairy tales for the little one. He keeps the house warm by managing a thermostat. He greets guests when they stay with us and helps them set up WiFi and can answer their questions about the neighborhood. In some way it's just a home assistant, but the physical presence, the back-story and the loyalty to place (our house) instead of individual humans is creating a very different kind of feeling around the whole thing.

The single-house Tomte is just one scale on which Nymph-like ASIs can be built. Imagine your public park having its own dryads. Imagine a city neighborhood having its own spirit whose sole objective is to make this neighborhood (and its inhabitants) thrive. Imagine your car having its own Kami. Imaging coming to a public library and checking in with its ghost, who is a thousand years old and has seen generations of students and scholars come and go.

One last thing to mention about Nymph-like ASIs is that the place they are loyal to doesn't have to be physical. A Discord server could be a place that can have its own Nymph too. Or a company could have its Nymph, that can outlive the current management. What would the job of such a company nymph be? In a dark scenario you could say that its job would be maximizing shareholder value, without any concern for the employees or externalities, but I don't think it has to be the case. Remember, a Nymph's primary concern is the holistic wellbeing of her habitat. If the company is her habitat - then her primary concern is keeping the company alive and thriving, not squeezing out profits for the shareholders. In the eyes of a Nymph, the shareholder value is a byproduct of a healthy, thriving company (as it should be).

In short, this is my message to the AI community: the signularity doesn't have to be singular. Instead of building Gods who are loyal only to principles (that few will define) or Genies who are loyal only individual persons (who will have unwise wishes), let us grow Dryads, who are loyal to places, whose fate is bound to them and whose job is to tend to them and their inhabitants. Let us build ASI on a human scale, so that we can relate to it. Let us make superintelligences that care about small places and long time horizons, about the small here and the big now, not the big here and the small now. Let us make creatures that will outlive us, but that won't tower over us.

Let us make Nymphs, not Gods.
